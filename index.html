About

I am a master's student in robotics and autonomous systems at Boston University, in the LEAP program. I build hardware for a living well before anyone pays me to.

What I look for in a project is the part most people buy. On the arm it was the reducer; on the jet engine it was the compressor; on the robot it was the middleware. Each time the decision costs me months and teaches me something a bought part never would.

I work across mechanical, electrical, and software rather than choosing one — because most of the interesting failures in a robot live at the boundaries between them.

IN PROGRESS

6-DOF arm with self-designed cycloidal reducers

Independent build · aluminium · BU EPIC

Integrated actuator modules were the easy answer and I turned them down. I am designing the cycloidal reducers, cutting the discs on wire EDM, and then measuring what I actually made — backlash, transmission error, efficiency, and torque ripple.

A bought actuator tells an employer I can read a datasheet. A reducer I designed, machined, and characterised tells them something else.

DESIGN STUDY

Axial-flow turbojet for small UAVs

Independent design study · six-phase roadmap, phase 1 complete

Almost every hobby-scale jet uses a centrifugal compressor because it is far easier to make. I am going fully axial. The whole architecture follows from what I can manufacture: a constant-tip-diameter annulus for straight-bore lathe work, and ruled-surface blisks cut on wire EDM.

Phase 1 complete — mean-line design off the Euler turbomachine equation, velocity triangles closed at the mean radius. Targeting 150–400 N.

BENCH TEST

Attitude-hold autopilot, fixed-wing aircraft

Independent build · Jetson Nano · BNO055 IMU

The aircraft already flies on manual control. I am adding an IMU-driven attitude-hold loop in C++ that takes over the control surfaces mid-flight, tested by suspending the airframe from the ceiling and checking that the surfaces answer a disturbance correctly before anything is risked in the air.
