RB-01BRING-UP

Autonomous differential-drive robot

Independent build · Jetson Orin · no ROS

Written from the bare metal up rather than assembled from a middleware stack. I drive the motor controller over raw I²C, decode quadrature counts straight off the register block, and close the velocity loop myself — so every layer between a commanded twist and a wheel turning is code I can account for.

Skipping ROS was deliberate. It costs me the ecosystem and buys me an understanding of odometry, timing and transport that I would not get from configuring someone else's nodes.

RB-02IN DESIGN

6-DOF arm with cycloidal reducers I design and cut myself

Independent build · aluminium · machined at BU EPIC

Integrated actuator modules were the easy answer and I turned them down. Instead I am designing the cycloidal reducers, cutting the discs on wire EDM, and then measuring what I actually made — backlash, transmission error, efficiency and torque ripple, on my own test fixture.

A bought actuator tells an employer I can read a datasheet. A reducer I designed, machined and characterised tells them I can close the loop between an assumption and a measurement.

PR-01DESIGN STUDY

Axial-flow turbojet for small UAVs

Independent design study · six-phase roadmap, phase 1 complete

Almost every hobby-scale jet uses a centrifugal compressor because it is far easier to make. I am going fully axial, and the whole architecture follows from what I can actually manufacture: a constant-tip-diameter annulus so the outer casing is a straight bore on a lathe, and ruled-surface blisks cut on wire EDM instead of a 5-axis mill I do not have.

Phase 1 is done — mean-line design off the Euler turbomachine equation with a work-done factor of 0.85–0.9, velocity triangles closed at the mean radius. This is a multi-year study, and I am describing it as one.

AV-01BENCH TEST

Attitude-hold autopilot for a fixed-wing aircraft

Independent build · airframe flown manually, autopilot in bench test

The aircraft already flies on manual control. What I am adding is the layer underneath: an IMU-driven attitude-hold loop in C++ that takes over the control surfaces mid-flight, tested first by suspending the airframe from the ceiling and checking that the surfaces answer a disturbance correctly before anything is risked in the air.

About

I am a master's student in robotics and autonomous systems at Boston University, in the LEAP program, and I build hardware for a living well before anyone pays me to.

What I look for in a project is the part most people buy. On the arm it was the reducer; on the jet engine it was the compressor; on the robot it was the middleware. Each time the decision costs me months and teaches me something a bought part never would — and each time I end up with a measurement I can defend rather than a spec sheet I have to trust.

I work across mechanical, electrical and software rather than choosing one, because most of the interesting failures in a robot live at the boundaries between them.
